# Overstating the Inaccessibility of Information Puts the Emphasis in the Right Place

Read literally, each statement of the law is too absolute. One does sometimes hold exactly the information a decision requires, so the objection that the wording is too black-or-white, too all-or-nothing, and too pessimistic is fair on its own terms.

The absolute wording still earns its place as emphasis rather than as an exact count. It marks which side of the gap between the information at hand and the information a decision requires deserves attention, without denying that the two occasionally coincide.
