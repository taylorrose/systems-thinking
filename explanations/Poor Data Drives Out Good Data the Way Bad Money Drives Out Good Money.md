# Poor Data Drives Out Good Data the Way Bad Money Drives Out Good Money

Gresham's Law observes that when bad money circulates alongside good, the bad money is what stays in use. Extended to systems generally, the same asymmetry appears wherever quality and quantity compete for the same channel.

Poor data is cheap and therefore plentiful, and a system with finite attention spends that attention on whatever arrives in bulk. Sound observations do not have to be refuted; they are simply crowded out of the space where a decision gets made.
