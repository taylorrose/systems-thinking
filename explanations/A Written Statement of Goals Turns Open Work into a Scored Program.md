# A Written Statement of Goals Turns Open Work into a Scored Program

A demand for goals and objectives does not simply ask what someone is doing. It requires a formal document — objectives ranked by priority, each with a time-frame and criteria for deciding whether it was achieved — and the form itself pushes toward goals that sound well-defined and important rather than toward the ones actually held.

Once submitted, that document is a program in writing. Achievement can be scored against it as a proportion: two of three promised papers is sixty-seven percent, and unplanned work counts for nothing however substantial it turns out to be.
