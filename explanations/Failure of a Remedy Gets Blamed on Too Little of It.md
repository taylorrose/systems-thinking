# Failure of a Remedy Gets Blamed on Too Little of It

When a reform fails to produce the desired effect, the failure invites delusional readings. A scapegoat may be blamed, but more commonly the shortfall is ascribed to lack of vigor in carrying the reform out, that is, to too little of the erroneous remedy having been applied. Each new catastrophe then stops being a signal that the policy is not working and becomes the occasion for a demand for redoubled vigor.
