# A System’s Expansion Has No Internal Stopping Point

Stated in its most general form, the tendency to expand carries no limit of its own: nothing inside a system marks the size at which it should stop, and no point of sufficiency is built into it. The limit belongs to the tendency's setting rather than to the tendency itself, which is why the most general formulation of the law names no bound at all.
