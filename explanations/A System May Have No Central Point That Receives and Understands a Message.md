# A System May Have No Central Point That Receives and Understands a Message

A message sent to a system may be seen by only one person in only one department, and that person may not understand it. The system as such can remain entirely ignorant that the message arrived, let alone of its content, while whatever response organs it has remain unactivated.

There is no guarantee of a central thinking brain that receives a message, processes it intelligently, routes it to the appropriate sub-centers, and answers. Edward T. Hall put the point more strongly, saying that bureaucracies by their nature have no conscience, no memory, and no mind; that may go too far, but the absence of a single comprehending recipient is the condition to prepare for.
