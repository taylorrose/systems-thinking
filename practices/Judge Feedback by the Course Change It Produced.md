# Judge Feedback by the Course Change It Produced

When a system claims to have a feedback loop, pick a specific piece of information it received and name what the system did differently afterward. State the change in terms of behavior — an action taken, stopped, or reversed — rather than in terms of reports written, meetings held, or acknowledgements sent.

If no behavioral difference can be named, record the channel as sensory input rather than feedback, and look for where the information stops short of the decision it was supposed to reach.
