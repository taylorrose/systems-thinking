# Separate What You Need to Know from What You Can Obtain

Before committing to a decision, state what the decision requires you to know. Then state separately what you can actually obtain within the time, access, and channels available to you.

Treat the difference between the two lists as a standing condition of the decision rather than a task that will be finished. Choose among the options that remain workable on obtainable information, and mark which parts of the choice rest on information you never got. Without that mark the gap disappears into the decision and can no longer be examined.
