# Ask What Question the Knowledge Is Meant to Answer

Before collecting, storing, or expanding a body of information, name the question it is meant to answer and the decision that answer would inform. Ask who would use it, when they would need it, and what they would do differently once they had it.

If no such question can be stated, treat the collection as unjustified for now rather than as a general good. Apply the same test to information already held: what is not serving a question is bulk, however large the store.
