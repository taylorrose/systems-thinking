# Stopping a System an Entire Population Depends On Costs More Than Expanding It

When a system's operation has become tied to the livelihoods of the people around it, dismantling it means imposing the shortfall on them directly, while continuing it defers that cost. Expansion can then be the cheaper course at each decision point, and the option of stopping recedes as more of the population's fate is bound up in the system's continued operation.
