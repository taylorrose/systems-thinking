# IF YOU’RE NOT THERE, THE ACCIDENT CAN HAPPEN WITHOUT YOU

[[explanations/Problem Avoidance Sidesteps a Problem That Offers No Toe-Hold|Problem avoidance sidesteps a problem that offers no toe-hold]]; for example, [[examples/Challenger Could Have Let the Ice Melt and Flown Another Day|Challenger could have let the ice melt and flown another day]], and [[examples/Napoleon Could Have Spared Himself by Not Marching on Moscow|Napoleon could have spared himself by not marching on Moscow]]. Moreover, [[explanations/Avoiding a Problem Is Initiative, Not Passivity|avoiding a problem is initiative, not passivity]]. However, [[effects/Successful Problem Avoidance Leaves No Record to Point At|successful problem avoidance leaves no record to point at]], and [[effects/A Missing Proverb Probably Explains Why Problem Avoidance Is Rarely Practiced|a missing proverb probably explains why problem avoidance is rarely practiced]]. Since [[effects/Avoiding a Problem Also Avoids the Wreckage a Failed Solution Leaves|avoiding a problem also avoids the wreckage a failed solution leaves]], [[practices/Ask Whether the Problem Can Be Avoided Before Engaging It|ask whether the problem can be avoided before engaging it]].
