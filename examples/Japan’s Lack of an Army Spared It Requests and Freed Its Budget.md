# Japan’s Lack of an Army Spared It Requests and Freed Its Budget

In the years after World War II, Japan had no standing army, and was therefore spared many tedious requests that such an army would have made possible. The savings to the national budget were, the author suggests, astronomical, and they helped Japan reach industrial parity with Western nations.
