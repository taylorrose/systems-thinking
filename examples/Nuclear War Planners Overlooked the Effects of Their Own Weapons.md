# Nuclear War Planners Overlooked the Effects of Their Own Weapons

In forty years of imagining nuclear war scenarios, defense planners seem to have forgotten the electromagnetic pulse, which at the first explosion would black out communications and short-circuit electrical installations across a continent. They also seem to have overlooked that nuclear explosions stir up dust, blotting out the sun and producing cold, darkness, and crop failure. Physicists realized only recently that detonating a warhead atop a nuclear power plant could leave a nation the size of England, France, or Germany largely uninhabitable for fifty years, while missiles are buried for protection and power plants are left on the surface near large cities.
