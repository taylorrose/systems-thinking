# The Anthropologist Becomes Part of the Family Being Studied

As anthropologists began to study tribes in their native habitats, they increasingly noticed that the typical New Guinea family consisted of father, mother, three children, and one or more anthropologists.

The observers had set out to record a household from outside it and ended as members of the household they were recording.
