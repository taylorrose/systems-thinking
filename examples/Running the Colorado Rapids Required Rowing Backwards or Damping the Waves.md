# Running the Colorado Rapids Required Rowing Backwards or Damping the Waves

The history of attempts to master the rapids of the Colorado River in the Grand Canyon produced only two methods with a reasonable chance of survival. The Galloway Technique consists entirely of entering the rapids backwards, stern first, and pulling upstream with all available strength. The later advance was the very large rubber raft, which in effect damps out the wildly fluctuating waves. Each is a paradox or a narrow specialization rather than a general improvement in boating.
