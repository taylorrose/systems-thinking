# A Test Animal Under Controlled Conditions Behaves as It Pleases

In the 1950s a group of biologists summarized their laboratory experience in what became known as the Harvard Law of Animal Behavior: under precisely controlled experimental conditions, a test animal will behave as it damn well pleases. Controlling every condition the experimenters could name did not make the animal's behavior follow from those conditions.
