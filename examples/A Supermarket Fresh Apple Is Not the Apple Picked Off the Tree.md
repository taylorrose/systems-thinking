# A Supermarket Fresh Apple Is Not the Apple Picked Off the Tree

On a farm in the right season you walk to the orchard and pick a dead-ripe apple off the tree. The neighborhood grocer brings apples in bushel baskets from an orchard twenty miles away, less fresh and with the best of the lot already sold to gift houses. The supermarket chain picks them green, ripens them in controlled-atmosphere storage, ships them for days or weeks in boxcars, and may finish the process with enzymes or exotic gases, so that only a few varieties survive. All three are called supplying a fresh apple.
