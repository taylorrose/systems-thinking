# A Course in How to Study Fails the Students Who Cannot Study

When a freshman fails several courses, it is usually assumed with reason that the student does not know how to study, so administrators set up a college course in HOW TO STUDY. The course follows the standard format: lectures, reading assignments, and a grade.

Substantial numbers of students predictably fail it, since not knowing how to study is why they are there. Because it carries credit, the standard rules apply: a student who fails HOW TO STUDY becomes entitled to the make-up course, REMEDIAL HOW TO STUDY, which also carries credit, and eventually to REMEDIAL REMEDIAL. Students and professor alike may feel trapped in a hall of mirrors of infinite regress, with a system designed to help them now holding them in perpetual failure. That the regress is not fanciful is suggested by a 1983 report finding that remedial courses had grown to a quarter of all college mathematics.
