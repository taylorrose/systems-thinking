# American White Bread Would Not Be Recognized as Bread in Most of the World

Cheap, universally available enriched white bread looks like a systems-achievement in nourishing a population. Yet a French country-dweller or an Egyptian fellah would scarcely recognize it: bread in most of the world is baked locally from regionally grown whole grain flour and sold still hot from the oven, features sacrificed to mass marketing requirements. Calling the replacement of components lost in earlier manufacture “enrichment” seems a misnomer, and the long-term effects of the chemicals added to retard spoilage and alter texture are, charitably, conjectural.
