# A Fungus That Ruined Fleming’s Cultures Became Antibiotics Once Reframed

A Penicillium fungus repeatedly spoiled Dr. Fleming's attempts to grow bacteria in Petri dishes, and by every working standard it was a contaminant to be excluded. The behaviour did not change when he stopped describing it as a bug and described it instead as an unsuspected behavioural capability; the world of antibiotics dates from that moment of redescription.
